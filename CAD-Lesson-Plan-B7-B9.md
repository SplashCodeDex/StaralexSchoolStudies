# Creative Arts & Design — Lesson Plan (Grades 7–9)

*Based on the NaCCA Creative Arts and Design Common Core Programme (CCP) Curriculum for JHS1 (B7) – JHS3 (B9).*

**How the subject is grouped.** NaCCA builds CAD from four disciplines: Design, Visual Arts, Music, and Dance & Drama. This plan keeps **three groups** — **Visual Arts** (which carries the Design lessons), **Music** and **Dance Drama**.

**Time allocation (per week, 4 periods of 50 minutes each).** Design — 1 period. Visual Arts — 2 periods. Music and Dance–Drama — alternate on the remaining period.

**Note.** NaCCA expects CAD to be **taught and evaluated practically**. Learners must observe, listen, reflect, brainstorm, compose, perform, report and appraise. Theory is kept to a minimum — only to reinforce ideation, conceptualisation and critical thinking.

# VISUAL ARTS

## Strand: Design *(1 period a week)*

### Sub-strand: Design in Nature and the Manmade Environment

**Grade 7**

1. Design in nature and the manmade environment — meaning, importance and how nature inspires manmade designs
2. Elements of design — dots, lines and shapes
3. Principles of design — balance, rhythm and repetition

**Grade 8**

1. Using the elements and principles of design as a medium of creative expression
2. Elements of design — texture and colour
3. Principles of design — unity and variety

**Grade 9**

1. Design as a concept — elements and principles at the deeper level
2. Elements of design — form and value
3. Principles of design — variety and proportion

*Notice:* Design is graded across the years — Grade 7: dots, lines, shapes (balance, rhythm, repetition); Grade 8: texture, colour (unity, variety); Grade 9: form, value (variety, proportion). Classic links: the kingfisher's beak inspired the Shinkansen Bullet Train; the anthill inspired the Eastgate Building in Zimbabwe.

### Sub-strand: Drawing, Shading, Colouring and Modelling for Design

**Grade 7** *(from lines and simple shapes)*

1. Manual tools, materials and techniques for outline drawing, shading and colouring (T-square, set square, protractor, pencil, paper)
2. Digital tools for drawing, shading and colouring, and exhibiting a portfolio
3. Generating design ideas and making models of simple shapes and forms (cardboard, Styrofoam, clay, pulp paper)

**Grade 8** *(same lessons, built on 2-D drawing)*

**Grade 9** *(same lessons, built on 3-D drawing)*

*Notice:* The same three lessons repeat each year, but the work deepens: Grade 7 – freehand/outline, Grade 8 – 2-D drawing, Grade 9 – 3-D drawing.

### Sub-strand: Creativity, Innovation and the Design Process

**Grades 7, 8 and 9** *(each year revisits these three lessons)*

1. Creativity versus innovation — and design solutions that solve society's problems
2. The design process — define, brief, research, ideation, invent, prototype, test, refine
3. Applying the design process to make artefacts that solve problems in the local community

*Notice:* Examples of design solutions: prosthetics, wheelchairs, hearing aids (Grade 7); energy conservation, energy efficiency (Grade 9). Designs to examine locally: the cutlass in farming, kitchen stool, farming basket.

## Strand: Creative Arts — Visual Arts

### Sub-strand: Media and Techniques

**Grade 7** *(still-life drawing, pattern making, modelling)*

1. Tools, materials and techniques for still-life drawing and shading, pattern making and modelling — and their safe use, cleaning and storage
2. Still-life drawing and shading techniques — hatching, cross-hatching, smoothing, mass shading, stippling
3. Preparing and modelling with clay and paper pulp (papier-mâché) — coiling, slab building, pinch pot
4. Pattern making techniques — dabbing, sponging, spraying, stippling, wax-resist/crayon batik, marbling

**Grade 8** *(observation/memory/imagination drawing, print making, weaving)*

1. Media and techniques for drawing from direct observation, memory and imagination; print making and weaving — safe use, maintenance and storage
2. Print making — relief, direct, stencil and block printing
3. Drawing from direct observation, memory and imagination
4. Weaving — plain weave, twill and satin, using cane, raffia, yarns, reeds

**Grade 9** *(casting, assemblage, folding, construction)*

1. Media and techniques for casting, assemblage and folding — safe use, maintenance and storage
2. Casting — filled-in, hump and hollow techniques
3. Folding — creasing, pleating, knotting (paper, fabric, ribbons)
4. Construction with found objects — stitching, gluing, stapling

*Notice:* Grade 9 construction artists to study — K. Z. Adzraku, Margaret Asabea Anakwa (local); P. M. Amonoo (national); El Anatsui, Ibrahim Mahama (international).

### Sub-strand: Creative and Aesthetic Expression

**Grade 7** *(artworks reflecting local history and culture)*

1. Design and produce own visual artworks that reflect the history and culture of the local community
2. Planning a display of own and others' artworks — keeping a portfolio, exhibiting in class, school and community
3. Appreciation and appraisal of own and others' artworks using art language (colour, form, tone, pattern, texture)

**Grade 8** *(artworks reflecting different times and cultures)*

1. Design and produce own artworks that reflect a range of different times and cultures — history, topical issues, natural and manmade environment
2. Plan and display own and others' artworks — roles and responsibilities for an exhibition
3. Appreciation and appraisal of artworks of the community's history, culture and environment

**Grade 9** *(exhibiting artworks from philosophies of different times and cultures)*

1. Design and produce own artworks from the philosophies, designs and processes of different times and cultures
2. Plan and display artworks — organising a full exhibition (audience, venue, finance, publicity)
3. Appreciation and appraisal of artworks with class exhibition and self- and peer-review

### Sub-strand: Connections in Local and Global Cultures

**Grade 7** *(artworks of the community)*

1. Narrating own views of the community's history, culture, environment and topical issues (chieftaincy, festivals, landmarks, tourist sites)
2. Identifying local visual artworks that reflect history, culture and the environment — paintings, carvings, pottery, textiles, basketry, jewellery

**Grade 8** *(artworks of visual artists)*

1. Identifying and discussing visual artists whose works reflect the community — life, work and influence
2. Comparing and contrasting artworks of visual artists (e.g. Ablade Glover, Kofi Antobam, Theodosia Okoh, Francis Boateng)
3. Determining the design approaches and processes used by visual artists (e.g. Serge Attukwei Clottey turning plastic waste into art)

**Grade 9** *(artworks of African artists)*

1. Identifying, discussing and analysing creative artworks of African visual artists and how they solve continental issues
2. Examining how African visual artists connect with other creative arts and subjects in Africa

*Notice:* Grade 9 African artists to study include Ablade Glover, Dorothy Amenuke and Saka Aquaye.

# MUSIC

## Strand: Creative Arts — Music

### Sub-strand: Media and Techniques

**Grade 7** *(scale, note values, simple time)*

1. Singing the diatonic major scale in pitch, using solfège or John Curwen's hand signs
2. Note durational values — identifying symbols and performing their values against the semibreve by clapping
3. Conducting songs in simple duple, triple and quadruple time

*Notice:* Learners also place the seven letter names (A–G) of the C major scale on the lines and spaces of the treble clef.

**Grade 8** *(tempo, dynamics, simple forms)*

1. Tempo — how fast or slow music is heard (allegro, poco a poco, largo) and fast/slow music on specific occasions
2. Dynamics — the benefits of soft and loud music (piano, forte, crescendo, diminuendo)
3. Simple musical forms heard aurally — AB, ABA, AA, AABA, ABAB and ABC (binary, ternary, through-composed; verse-chorus song forms)

**Grade 9** *(triads, chord progression, improvisation)*

1. Singing triads on all seven degrees of the scale — horizontally as arpeggios and vertically in harmony
2. Creating and improvising a melody over the I, VI, IV and V ostinato bass line

### Sub-strand: Creative and Aesthetic Expression

**Grade 7** *(musical works of the community)*

1. Creating and producing own musical works that reflect the community's history and culture — indigenous genres expressing moods, images and storylines
2. Planning a display of own and others' musical works — a recorded class concert with a presentation on style and context
3. Appreciation and appraisal of own and others' musical works — collecting audience feedback and writing an appreciation report

**Grade 8** *(musical works from beyond the community)*

1. Designing and producing own musical genre reflecting different times and cultures, history and environment
2. Planning and displaying own and others' musical works — a recorded class concert
3. Appreciation and appraisal of own and others' musical works

**Grade 9** *(musical works of the African continent)*

1. Designing and producing own musical work on emerging topical issues — works of indigenous, art or pop musicians across Africa
2. Planning and displaying own and others' musical works — a concert on topical issues (climate change, sanitation, pension, etc.)
3. Appreciation and appraisal of own and others' musical works across Africa

### Sub-strand: Connections in Local and Global Cultures

**Grade 7** *(musicians in the community)*

1. Identifying indigenous and art musicians whose works reflect history, culture and topical issues (e.g. Agya Koo Nimo, Kakraba Lobi, E. T. Mensah, Kojo Antwi, Amakye Dede)
2. Appreciating and appraising an indigenous and a neo-traditional group — style, instruments, song themes, dance movements

**Grade 8** *(Ghanaian art musicians)*

1. Distinguishing how musical works of Ghanaian art composers reflect history and culture (e.g. Ephraim Amu and J. H. K. Nketia)
2. Appreciating and appraising three indigenous and neo-traditional groups within Ghana

**Grade 9** *(African art composers and genres)*

1. Distinguishing how musical works of African art composers reflect history and culture (e.g. Akin Euba and J. H. K. Nketia)
2. Appreciating and appraising three African indigenous dance genres and three popular musical genres — and writing an appreciation report on an African movie (acting, drama, music and dance scenes)

# DANCE DRAMA

## Strand: Creative Arts — Dance and Drama

### Sub-strand: Media and Techniques

**Grade 7** *(voice and movement techniques)*

1. Ghanaian dance and body movements, positions and voice projection patterns — physical exercises, rhythmic/theatre games, facial expressions and gestures
2. Techniques of dance, body movement and voice projection in the Ghanaian performance space — body profiles, postures, characterisation, low/medium/high dance levels

**Grade 8** *(Ghanaian dance forms)*

1. Identifying Ghanaian dance types and their context of performance — religious, war and socio-cultural categories
2. Rhythm (beat, tempo, intensity) and dynamics (fast, slow, smooth, flow) in dance and drama — choreographing a dance or directing a play

**Grade 9** *(media and techniques of dance drama)*

1. Creating a simple Dance Drama and/or a one-act play on a topical issue
2. Approaches to improvisation, harmony/balance and blocking — parts of the performance space, essential body parts and props, masking, aside, apron, linear and circular patterns
3. Practising improvisation and blocking — stage movements (DR, UL, DC) and stage positions ( profiles: open, close, full back, front; positions 1st–4th)

*Notice:* The Teacher's Resource Pack supplies the performance space diagrams for Grade 9 blocking lessons.

### Sub-strand: Creative and Aesthetic Expression

**Grade 7** *(performances of the community)*

1. Designing and producing own dance piece and drama skit reflecting community history and culture
2. Planning a performance — choosing a venue, spreading information, rehearsing and recording
3. Appreciation and appraisal of own and others' performances — noting strengths and weaknesses for improvement

**Grade 8** *(performances of own and other cultures)*

1. Designing and producing own dance and drama reflecting different times and cultures (drafting rehearsals around a real community issue)
2. Planning and displaying own and others' dance and drama pieces — preparing a venue and performing
3. Appreciation and appraisal of dance and drama artworks

**Grade 9** *(producing a dance drama)*

1. Performing an original dance drama and/or one-act play on socio-cultural issues (e.g. sanitation, energy, pension)
2. Post-performance analysis — reflecting on audience response and areas of improvement
3. Appreciation and appraisal of own and others' dance drama artworks

### Sub-strand: Connections in Local and Global Cultures

**Grade 7** *(dancers and actors in the community)*

1. Selecting artworks of dance and drama artistes and identifying the history, culture, environment and topical issues reflected in them
2. Analysing works of other dance and drama artistes — reviewing video clips and generating ideas from them

**Grade 8** *(artistes of own and other cultures)*

1. Selecting and analysing creative artworks of dance and drama artistes for documentation
2. Organising a group discussion to appreciate dances and/or plays of own and other cultures

**Grade 9** *(African dance and drama)*

1. Reflecting on a selected African creative work (dance and/or drama) and appreciating the ideas embedded in it
2. Creating and performing a one-act play or dance based on the selected work
3. Conducting a performance review — live performance or viewing the recording for appraisal
