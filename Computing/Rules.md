## Subject notes (Computing / ICT, B7–B9)

- Lesson body is **Theory Questions and Answers**: `Q. …? Ans. …` one sentence, or a bulleted list when the question asks for a list. Never bundle several questions into one.
- Exercises must be **BECE-shaped**: Paper 1 Objective (MCQ A–D, letter + reason) and Paper 2 Theory (marks + marking points).
- **Every** authentic bank question under the strand/sub-strand goes into the drills. Expand the quota rather than dropping an authentic question.
- **Grade scope distinctions:**
  - **Grade 7:** 4th-Gen computers, manual/optical input, plotters/projectors, magnetic/optical storage, Windows GUI & file management, green upcycling, Word/PPT/Excel basics, topologies, CIA triad, CRAAP, block programming, linear search, robotics & everyday AI.
  - **Grade 8:** 5th-Gen computers, Direct Data Entry (RFID/MICR), Specialist Output (Braille/3D printers), Flash/SSD storage, Charms & drive partitioning, Assistive Tech, **Desktop Publishing (introduced in B8)**, Excel statistical functions & `$` referencing, OSI/data comm models, 4 data threats, Boolean search, operators, Sense-Think-Act & ANNs.
  - **Grade 9:** Next-Gen/Quantum supercomputing, perceptual computing, E-Paper/wearables, cloud/hybrid storage, device drivers, community problem-solving & workstation audits, Word SmartArt/multimedia, PPT animations, multi-panel DTP, Excel sorting/filtering/validation/charts/Pivot Tables, e-commerce/MoMo, e-learning, 10 hacking techniques, blogs, binary Base 10 ↔ Base 2, flowcharts/pseudocode, robot fabrication & Expert Systems.
- Diagrams are **contextual** — add one only when the concept needs it (computer components, workstation posture, network topologies, DDE hardware, Publisher frame layouts, "identify the parts" figures). Never decorate.
- Scenario openings (the *Mr. Agyekum…* style) belong in the exercise block only, and only when the real bank question uses one.
- Plain simple English, British notation. Preambles and "words to know first" are mandatory.
- 2-month pace, 1–2 sub-strands per day. Never rush. Split long sub-strands across days.
