# Computing — Lesson Plan (Grades 7–9)

*Based on the NaCCA Computing Common Core Programme (CCP) Curriculum for JHS1 (B7) – JHS3 (B9).*

**How the subject is grouped.** NaCCA structures Computing into four major sessions (Main Strands):
1. **Introduction to Computing** — Computer hardware architecture, input/output/storage devices, operating system interfaces, file management, technology in the community, and health/workstation safety.
2. **Productivity Software** — Practical application software: Word Processing, Presentation, Desktop Publishing (introduced in B8), and Electronic Spreadsheets.
3. **Communication Networks** — Network architectures, transmission media, Internet/WWW, social media communication, information security/threats, cyber law, and web technologies/blogging.
4. **Computational Thinking** — Foundations of computer science: visual and text-based programming, data types, algorithmic logic (sequence, selection, iteration, linear search, flowcharts, pseudocode), robotics (Sense-Think-Act), and Artificial Intelligence (neural networks, expert systems).

**Time allocation (per week, 3–4 periods of 50 minutes each).** 1–2 periods for theory and conceptual foundations; 2 periods dedicated to hands-on computer laboratory practicals.

**Note.** NaCCA mandates Computing to be **practical and inquiry-led**. Learners must physically interact with hardware components, navigate live operating systems, type and format real documents and publications, build spreadsheets with working formulas, draw network diagrams, simulate algorithms, program microcontrollers/sprites, and evaluate real-world digital risks. Theory serves strictly to anchor hands-on execution and critical problem solving.

# STRAND 1: INTRODUCTION TO COMPUTING

## Strand 1: Introduction to Computing *(Hardware, OS, Community & Safety)*

### Sub-strand 1: Components of Computers and Computer Systems

**Grade 7** *(4th Generation, Standard I/O, Optical/Magnetic Storage, Windows GUI & File Management)*

1. Fourth-generation computers — microprocessors, microchips, Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI), and CPU architecture
2. Manual and automatic input devices — wireless keyboard, mouse, touchscreens, optical barcode readers, and flatbed scanners
3. Output devices — graphing plotters, data projectors, multimedia projectors, and pico projectors
4. Secondary storage devices — external hard disk drives (HDD speeds, disk caching), portable drives, and optical storage (CD/DVD/Blu-ray: ROM, R, RW/RE)
5. Operating system desktop navigation — Windows Start screen/menu, live tiles, taskbar buttons, preview thumbnails, and temporal window peeking (Aero Peek)
6. File and folder management — creating, naming, renaming, moving, copying, deleting, and structuring hierarchical folders

**Grade 8** *(5th Generation, Direct Data Entry, Solid-State Storage, Charms & Drive Management)*

1. Fifth-generation computers — artificial intelligence integration, ultra-large-scale integration (ULSI), and parallel processing hardware
2. Direct Data Entry (DDE) devices — magnetic stripe readers, chip and PIN readers, Radio Frequency Identification (RFID), and Magnetic Ink Character Recognition (MICR)
3. Specialist output devices — Braille printers, 3D printers, and wearable heads-up displays
4. Solid-state and flash memory storage — USB flash drives, SD/microSD memory cards, and Solid-State Drives (SSDs) versus HDDs
5. OS navigation tools — Windows Charms bar, charms search, system settings, and quick notification centers
6. Drive management and storage maintenance — drive partitioning, drive letters, disk formatting, storage cleanup, and disk defragmentation

**Grade 9** *(Next-Gen Trends, Perceptual Computing, Wearables/Cloud, Personalisation & Device Drivers)*

1. Next-generation computer trends — quantum computing concepts, Google Quantum AI Sycamore processor, and supercomputing capabilities
2. Perceptual computing — natural user interfaces, voice recognition, facial authentication, gesture control, and gaze tracking
3. Modern output technologies — wearable displays (e.g. smart glasses, AR/VR displays), electronic paper (E-Paper), and e-book readers (Amazon Kindle)
4. Network and cloud storage systems — cloud storage architecture (Google Drive, OneDrive), smart cards, holographic data storage, and Network-Attached Storage (NAS) / media servers
5. Desktop personalisation — customising desktop icons, mouse pointers, themes, taskbar docking, and accessibility display options
6. Device drivers and hardware integration — device driver functions (graphics, audio), plug-and-play (PnP) hardware, installing, updating, and rolling back drivers

*Notice:* Hardware progresses across computing eras: Generation 4 (B7) to Generation 5 (B8) to Quantum & Perceptual computing (B9). Input shifts from manual/optical scanners to direct automated cards/RFID, and finally to perceptual/biometric interfaces. Storage advances from spinning magnetic/optical discs to solid-state flash memory, and finally to distributed cloud/holographic repositories.

### Sub-strand 2: Technology in the Community (Communication)

**Grade 7** *(Community Technology Identification, Learning Tools, E-Waste & Upcycling)*

1. Technology tools in the community — identifying and categorising at least five common tools (smartphones, ATMs, point-of-sale terminals, traffic light controllers, automated fuel dispensers)
2. Operating community technology tools — practical operation of at least three common community tools
3. Educational benefits of technology — learning management platforms, educational apps, research access, and virtual collaboration
4. Negative impacts of computers — electronic waste hazards, sedentary lifestyles, computer vision strain, and social isolation
5. Environmentally responsible practices (Green Computing) — safe disposal of e-waste, power management, device refurbishment, and recycling protocols
6. Creative upcycling project — designing and constructing a functional component or model using discarded computer scrap parts

**Grade 8** *(Assistive Technologies, Portable Devices & Online Service Challenges)*

1. Assistive technologies in the community — tools enabling persons with disabilities to use computing systems (screen readers, braille displays, sip-and-puff switches, speech-to-text engines)
2. Impact of portable computing devices — smartphones, tablets, and laptops transforming everyday commerce, personal communication, and social interaction
3. Issues associated with online services — electronic banking risks, mobile money fraud, network latency, data privacy breaches, and digital surveillance

**Grade 9** *(Community Problem Identification, Solution Formulation & Design Prototyping)*

1. Evaluating community problems — investigating real local challenges in agriculture, sanitation, education, or market commerce that can be addressed via technology
2. Proposing technological solutions — brainstorming, selecting, and justifying digital solutions (mobile applications, automated alerts, SMS broadcast systems)
3. Solution design and prototyping — mapping system architectures, flow diagrams, user journeys, and structural mock-ups of the selected community solution

*Notice:* Progresses from recognising community tools, adopting green computing, and building scrap models (B7) to examining assistive access and online commercial risks (B8), culminating in an authentic engineering design challenge where learners identify a local community problem, evaluate options, and design a technology prototype (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 3: Health and Safety in the Use of ICT Tools

**Grade 7** *(Ergonomics, Electrical Safety, Lab Regulations & Physical Setup)*

1. Health measures and regulatory requirements — ergonomics, correct seating posture, Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI), Computer Vision Syndrome (eyestrain), and national safety standards
2. Safety measures with ICT tools — safe power connections, cable management, surge suppression, avoiding overloaded sockets, and fire prevention
3. Computer laboratory safety procedures — lab conduct, dust prevention, ventilation, liquid prohibition, and emergency evacuation protocols
4. Workstation safety assessment — adjusting chair height, monitor eye level, keyboard positioning, and ambient room illumination

**Grade 8** *(Workstation Risk Assessment & Physical Fatigue Mitigation)*

1. Workstation health risk assessment — identifying physical and psychological fatigue factors (lumbar stress, cervical strain, chronic headaches, acoustic fatigue)
2. Risk reduction practices at workstations — ergonomic adjustments, anti-glare screen filters, wrist rests, footrests, and implementing the 20-20-20 visual rest routine

**Grade 9** *(Health Compliance Auditing & Institutional Safety Protocol Design)*

1. Evaluating workstation health compliance — auditing classroom, school, or office computer setups against occupational ergonomics guidelines
2. Developing comprehensive safety protocols — designing institutional hazard mitigation plans, workstation risk-reduction policies, and lab emergency manuals

*Notice:* Progresses from personal ergonomic habits and lab rules (B7) to identifying occupational hazard patterns and practicing active risk reduction (B8), concluding with auditing institutional compliance and authoring safety policy frameworks (B9).

# STRAND 2: PRODUCTIVITY SOFTWARE

## Strand 2: Productivity Software *(Office Applications & Document Publishing)*

### Sub-strand 1: Introduction to Word Processing

**Grade 7** *(Basic Word Processing, Text Editing, Proofing, Typography & Paragraph Layout)*

1. Importance of word processing — role in document preparation versus manual typewriting
2. Text editing operations — selecting, inserting, deleting, cutting, copying, pasting, and moving text
3. Content search and navigation — Find and Replace commands, Undo and Redo actions
4. Document proofing tools — Spell Check, Grammar Check, and the Thesaurus
5. Character formatting and typography — font families, font sizes, font colors, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, subscript, and superscript
6. Paragraph alignment and spacing — left, center, right, justified alignment, line spacing, and paragraph indentation (first line, hanging)
7. Tabs and borders — setting left, right, center, and decimal tab stops; applying paragraph borders and shading

**Grade 8** *(Tables, Formulas, Hyperlinks & Page Layout)*

1. Table creation and formatting — inserting tables, inserting/deleting rows and columns, merging cells, splitting cells, and table border styling
2. Formulas in Word tables — calculating table values using functions (e.g. `=SUM()`, `=AVERAGE()`)
3. Hyperlinks and non-linear documents — creating hyperlinks to web addresses, bookmarks, and local documents
4. Page layout setup — adjusting page margins, setting portrait/landscape orientation, paper sizes, page breaks, section breaks, columns, headers, footers, and page numbering

**Grade 9** *(Graphics, SmartArt, Multimedia Integration & Document Compilation)*

1. Inserting visual assets — inserting images, cropping pictures, applying picture styles, and capturing screenshots directly into documents
2. SmartArt graphics — inserting and configuring visual relationship diagrams (process flows, hierarchies, cyclical diagrams, lists)
3. Multimedia integration — embedding audio clips, video clips, and live interactive web links into digital documents
4. Comprehensive publication project — compiling a multi-page, formatted project report combining tables, SmartArt diagrams, illustrations, and styled headers

*Notice:* Progresses from elementary typing, editing, and typography formatting (B7) to tabular data, table math, hyperlinks, and formal page layout (B8), culminating in multimedia integration, SmartArt concept mapping, and publishing professional reports (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 2: Introduction to Presentation (Software)

**Grade 7** *(PowerPoint Interface, Text Controls, Slide Masters & Design Templates)*

1. Importance of presentation software — applications in lectures, seminars, business pitches, and visual storytelling
2. Presentation workspace interface — Ribbon tabs, Slide Thumbnail pane, Slide Canvas, Notes pane, Status bar, and View modes (Normal, Slide Sorter, Reading, Slide Show)
3. Text input and special characters — text boxes, character formatting, case conversion, font resizing, and inserting mathematical/special symbols
4. Paragraph formatting on slides — text alignment, line spacing, bulleted lists, numbered lists, and borders
5. Slide Master and design themes — applying built-in design themes, modifying slide background styles, and customising layout templates using the Slide Master

**Grade 8** *(Media Assets, Shapes, Canvases & Object Manipulation)*

1. Images and screen captures — inserting local pictures, online images, capturing screenshots, and applying image cropping/enhancement
2. Drawing canvas and shapes — drawing geometric shapes, banners, callouts, lines, connectors, and using drawing canvases
3. Text within shapes and object arrangement — typing text inside shapes, grouping/ungrouping shapes, aligning objects, rotating, and managing layer stacking order (Bring to Front, Send to Back)

**Grade 9** *(Slide Animation, Transitions, Multimedia & Delivery Skills)*

1. Object animations — configuring entrance, emphasis, exit, and motion path animation sequences with custom speed and trigger timings
2. Slide transition effects — applying transition styles between slides, setting sound effects, transition durations, and slide advance timers
3. Multimedia embedding — embedding background audio tracks, voiceover recordings, video clips, and action navigation buttons
4. Professional slide delivery — presenting using Presenter View, managing speaker notes, pacing visual aids, and avoiding slide clutter

*Notice:* Progresses from mastering slide layout, typography, and global Slide Masters (B7) to technical vector shape drafting, grouping, and layering (B8), finishing with dynamic motion choreography (transitions/animations), multimedia synchronization, and professional presentation delivery (B9).

### Sub-strand 3: Introduction to Desktop Publishing (DTP)

*Note: In accordance with the NaCCA CCP scope and sequence, Desktop Publishing begins in Basic 8 and deepens in Basic 9.*

**Grade 7** *(Not offered in B7 — students master basic document processing prior to desktop publishing).*

**Grade 8** *(MS Publisher Environment, Layout Guides, Text Frames, Picture Handling & Basic Publications)*

1. Importance of desktop publishing — comparing DTP software (MS Publisher) with standard word processors for frame-based page composition
2. Document creation and templates — initiating blank publications and adapting built-in templates (flyers, brochures, greeting cards, banners)
3. MS Publisher interface and core tools — Page Navigation pane, scratch area, rulers, margin guides, and layout grids
4. Page setup and orientation — setting custom dimensions, orientation, margin boundaries, and master layout guidelines
5. Picture handling in DTP — importing graphics, picture placeholders, text wrapping configurations (tight, square, through), framing, and cropping
6. Text frame typography — drawing text boxes, linking overflow text frames, drop caps, and font stylings
7. Designing and presenting simple publications — producing and exhibiting a one-page promotional flyer, event invitation, or business card

**Grade 9** *(Multi-Page Publication Production, Technical Anatomy & Design Critique)*

1. Multi-panel and advanced publications — designing complex publications: three-fold brochures, multi-page school newsletters, certificates, and commercial notices
2. Structural description of DTP documents — analyzing balance, visual hierarchy, focal points, white space usage, color harmony, and typographic consistency
3. Critical appraisal and evaluation — evaluating student and commercial publications against audience appropriateness, message clarity, aesthetic balance, and printing specifications

*Notice:* Introduced in B8 to establish frame-based graphic composition, text frame chaining, and single-page publishing. B9 expands this into multi-panel layouts (brochures/newsletters) and rigorous critical appraisal against commercial design principles.

### Sub-strand 4: Introduction to Electronic Spreadsheet

*Note: Listed as Sub-strand 3 in Basic 7; becomes Sub-strand 4 in Basic 8 and 9 alongside Desktop Publishing.*

**Grade 7** *(Spreadsheet Grid, Data Types, Formatting, Page Setup, Autofill & Simple Formulas)*

1. Importance of electronic spreadsheets — automated computation, tabular data organization, inventory tracking, and financial analysis
2. MS Excel interface navigation — Workbooks, worksheets, rows, columns, cell addresses, Name Box, Formula Bar, and sheet tabs
3. Cell data types and number formatting — text, numbers, dates, time, currency values, percentages, and decimal control
4. Cell alignment and formatting — text orientation, Merge & Center, Wrap Text, cell borders, and background fills
5. Page layout and print setup — setting print margins, portrait/landscape orientation, scaling to fit, print area boundaries, headers, and footers
6. Autofill and sequences — using the Fill Handle to generate number series, calendar dates, months, days of the week, and custom lists
7. Basic formula creation — formula syntax (starting with `=`), arithmetic operators (`+`, `-`, `*`, `/`), and basic cell calculations (e.g. `=A1+B1`)

**Grade 8** *(Built-in Functions, Complex Expressions, Cell Referencing & Error Debugging)*

1. Built-in statistical and mathematical functions — syntax and application of `SUM`, `AVERAGE`, `COUNT`, `COUNTA`, `MAX`, and `MIN`
2. Complex formulas and operator priority — building nested arithmetic formulas using parentheses and standard BODMAS/PEMDAS order of operations
3. Cell referencing techniques — relative referencing (`A1`), absolute referencing (`$A$1`), and mixed referencing (`$A1`, `A$1`)
4. Formula replication and error code resolution — copying formulas across cells and diagnosing standard spreadsheet error flags (`#DIV/0!`, `#VALUE!`, `#REF!`, `#####`, `#NAME?`)

**Grade 9** *(Data Sorting/Filtering, Data Validation, Themes, Charts & Pivot Tables)*

1. Data sorting and filtering — ascending and descending sorts across single and multiple columns; basic and custom auto-filters
2. Data validation controls — establishing input restriction criteria (whole numbers, date ranges, decimal limits), drop-down choice lists, and custom input/error alert messages
3. Spreadsheet styling and conditional formatting — applying cell styles, table formatting styles, and conditional formatting rules (data bars, color scales, highlight cell rules)
4. Charting and visual data analysis — creating, configuring, and labelling column charts, bar charts, line graphs, and pie charts
5. Data aggregation with Pivot Tables — summarizing, grouping, and analyzing large datasets using Excel Pivot Tables

*Notice:* Progresses from data entry, cell data types, grid formatting, and simple addition/multiplication formulas (B7) to statistical functions, absolute cell locks (`$`), and error diagnostics (B8), concluding with dynamic data management tools: validation gates, sorting/filtering, statistical charts, and Pivot Table reports (B9).

# STRAND 3: COMMUNICATION NETWORKS

## Strand 3: Communication Networks *(Architecture, Internet, Cybersecurity & Web)*

### Sub-strand 1: Computer Networks

**Grade 7** *(Network Fundamentals, Topologies, Geographical Classifications & Entrepreneurship)*

1. Fundamentals of computer networks — definition, sharing hardware/software resources, data communication, and core hardware (nodes, network interface cards, switches, routers)
2. Network topology architectures — drawing, illustrating, and evaluating Bus, Star, Ring, Mesh, and Tree/Hybrid topologies
3. Network classification by geographical scope — Personal Area Network (PAN), Local Area Network (LAN), Metropolitan Area Network (MAN), and Wide Area Network (WAN)
4. Entrepreneurial opportunities in networking — network cable termination, Wi-Fi hotspot provision, local cyber café setup, and network equipment maintenance

**Grade 8** *(Communication Models, Transmission Media & Internet Architecture)*

1. Data communication models — sender, receiver, message payload, transmission medium, and communication protocols; transmission direction (simplex, half-duplex, full-duplex)
2. Transmission media — guided/wired channels (twisted-pair copper, coaxial cable, fiber-optic cable) versus unguided/wireless channels (radio waves, microwaves, infrared, satellite)
3. Structural architecture of the Internet — distinguishing the physical Internet from the World Wide Web (WWW); roles of Internet Service Providers (ISPs), IP addresses, Domain Name Systems (DNS), and URLs

**Grade 9** *(E-Commerce, Cashless Economy & E-Learning Systems)*

1. Electronic commerce and cashless societies — business models (B2B, B2C, C2C), online storefronts, electronic payment gateways, mobile money (MoMo), and digital transaction security
2. E-Learning platforms and potentials — distance education, Learning Management Systems (LMS: Moodle, Google Classroom), virtual classrooms, Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs), benefits, and local connectivity challenges

*Notice:* Progresses from structural topologies, geographical classifications, and hardware setups (B7) to physical/wireless transmission physics, communication models, and Internet routing architecture (B8), advancing to transformational economic network applications: mobile money, e-commerce, and e-learning platforms (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 2: Internet and Social Media

**Grade 7** *(Social Media Platforms, Email Features, Netiquette & Electronic Messaging)*

1. Types and uses of social media sites — social networks (Facebook, LinkedIn), media sharing (Instagram, YouTube, TikTok), microblogging (X/Twitter), and messaging (WhatsApp, Telegram)
2. Electronic mail (Email) operations — composing messages, email address structure, `To`, `Cc` (Carbon Copy), `Bcc` (Blind Carbon Copy), subject lines, file attachments, reply/forward protocols, and digital netiquette

**Grade 8** *(Collaborative Social Platforms, Academic Communities & Digital Footprints)*

1. In-depth social media analysis — online collaborative learning groups, professional networking, educational forums, and community development
2. Responsible digital citizenship — maintaining digital etiquette, managing personal privacy controls, identifying fake news/disinformation, and managing one's digital footprint

**Grade 9** *(Opportunities, Systemic Hazards & Online Harm Mitigation)*

1. Opportunities of social media — youth empowerment, creative enterprise, public advocacy, social commerce, and global knowledge exchange
2. Hazards and risk management — online identity theft, exposure to obscene content, cyber extortion, digital addiction, and actionable strategies for personal online safety

*Notice:* Progresses from basic social media classification and practical email mechanics (B7) to constructive collaborative online communities and digital footprint awareness (B8), culminating in critical risk assessment, counter-fraud strategies, and youth cyber safety (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 3: Information Security

**Grade 7** *(The CIA Triad, Intellectual Property, Digital Law & Forensic Auditing)*

1. Core principles of information security — the CIA Triad: Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability
2. Intellectual property and digital legal issues — copyright laws, software piracy, patents, trademarks, Fair Use guidelines, Creative Commons, proprietary versus open-source licensing, and national regulatory acts
3. Security auditing and forensic foundations — concept of forensic auditing, audit trails, digital evidence preservation, and why organisations conduct security audits

**Grade 8** *(Four Major Data Threats & Defense Mapping)*

1. Four major data threats — malicious software (viruses, worms, trojans, ransomware, spyware), unauthorized system penetration (hacking), data eavesdropping/interception, and accidental physical data destruction
2. Mapping defense countermeasures — antivirus and anti-malware tools, strong password creation, multi-factor authentication (MFA), data encryption techniques, network firewalls, and regular automated offsite data backups

**Grade 9** *(Interpersonal Cyber Harms & Ten Hacking Techniques)*

1. Cyber-victimization and digital footprints — identifying cyberbullying, cyberstalking, trolling, online harassment, non-consensual sharing, and preserving positive digital reputations
2. Ten (10) information hacking and attack techniques — detailed mechanics of phishing, spoofing, denial of service (DoS/DDoS), brute force cracking, man-in-the-middle (MitM), SQL injection, social engineering, network sniffing, keystroke logging (keyloggers), and DNS poisoning

*Notice:* Progresses from foundational security theory (CIA Triad), copyright laws, and forensic concepts (B7) to categorizing data threats and engineering defenses (antivirus, firewalls, MFA, encryption) (B8), reaching deep threat analysis of interpersonal cybercrimes and the technical mechanisms of ten major hacking vectors (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 4: Web Technologies

**Grade 7** *(Educational Web, Open Educational Resources & Web Evaluation)*

1. The World Wide Web in education — Virtual Learning Environments (VLEs), educational websites, online research portals, and interactive simulations
2. Open Educational Resources (OER) — locating, exploring, and applying open-access instructional materials and educational repositories in academic work
3. Web evaluation techniques — evaluating online information credibility using critical criteria: Authority, Accuracy, Objectivity, Currency, and Coverage (the CRAAP test methodology)

**Grade 8** *(Search Engine Query Syntax, Boolean Operators & Comparative Indexing)*

1. Advanced web searching techniques — formulating effective search queries, using search parameters, boolean operators (`AND`, `OR`, `NOT`), exact phrase matching (`" "`), exclusion flags (`-`), and domain filters (`site:`, `filetype:`)
2. Comparative analysis of search engines — comparing search indexing algorithms, user privacy practices, sponsored ad placements, and result rankings across Google, Bing, and DuckDuckGo

**Grade 9** *(Blogging Architecture, Development & Digital Publishing)*

1. Importance and function of blogs — weblogs as communication mediums, creative writing outlets, community bulletin boards, and educational repositories
2. Developing a school or club blog — creating and configuring a functional blog site using free blogging engines (Blogger, WordPress) with customized themes and pages
3. Publishing and maintaining web content — drafting blog posts, formatting embedded multimedia, moderating user comments, managing copyright, and publishing schedules

*Notice:* Progresses from consuming and critically evaluating web resources (B7) to mastering advanced search engine syntax and algorithmic comparison (B8), culminating in active web authorship through designing, building, and publishing an operational blog (B9).

# STRAND 4: COMPUTATIONAL THINKING

## Strand 4: Computational Thinking *(Programming, Algorithms, Robotics & AI)*

### Sub-strand 1: Introduction to Programming

**Grade 7** *(Programming Fundamentals, Block-Based Coding, Data Types, Variables & Constants)*

1. Programming fundamentals — definition of computer programs, programming languages (machine code, assembly, high-level languages), and block-based coding environments (Scratch, Blockly)
2. Fundamental data types — identifying and distinguishing Integer, Float/Real, String/Character, and Boolean data types
3. Variables and constants — defining variables (storage containers for dynamic values), constants (immutable values), naming conventions, and value assignment operations

**Grade 8** *(Programming Concepts, Operators, Expressions & State Tracing)*

1. Core programming constructs — declaring variables and constants, applying arithmetic operators (`+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, `%`), relational operators (`>`, `<`, `==`, `!=`, `>=`, `<=`), and logical operators (`AND`, `OR`, `NOT`)
2. Expressions and assignment statements — constructing algebraic and logic expressions, evaluating outputs, and dry-run tracing variable value states during program execution

**Grade 9** *(Binary Data Representation & Text-Based IDE Tools)*

1. Binary data representation — decimal to binary (Base 10 to Base 2) and binary to decimal conversion; representing alphanumeric characters, numbers, and machine logic in bits and bytes
2. Integrated Development Environments (IDEs) — exploring text-based development environments (Python IDLE, VS Code, online interpreters), source code editors, syntax highlighters, output terminals, and error consoles

*Notice:* Progresses from visual block programming, primitive data types, and variable concepts (B7) to mathematical/relational expressions and dry-run tracing (B8), before bridging to computer number systems (binary conversion) and professional text-based programming environments and IDEs (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 2: Algorithm

**Grade 7** *(Algorithmic Thinking, Sequence, Selection, Iteration & Linear Search)*

1. Algorithmic fundamentals — definition of an algorithm, properties of good algorithms (finiteness, precision, input/output specifications, effectiveness)
2. Fundamental control structures — Sequence (step-by-step linear flow), Selection (`IF-THEN-ELSE`), and Iteration/Looping (`FOR`, `WHILE`, `REPEAT-UNTIL`)
3. Linear search algorithm — tracing and dry-running a linear search on a list of items; identifying best, average, and worst-case search steps

**Grade 8** *(Variable Manipulation, Complex Conditions & Iterative Logic)*

1. Algorithmic variable assignments — writing structured statements to manipulate variables, accumulate totals, and swap values
2. Designing conditional and iterative algorithms — constructing multi-branch decision logic (`IF-THEN-ELSEIF`) and controlled loop structures to solve practical calculation problems

**Grade 9** *(Flowcharts, Structured Pseudocode & Bidirectional Translation)*

1. Flowchart and pseudocode modeling — standard flowchart symbols (terminal, input/output, process, decision, connector, flowline) and formal pseudocode keywords (`READ`, `WRITE`, `IF`, `ELSE`, `ENDIF`, `WHILE`, `ENDWHILE`)
2. Bidirectional algorithmic translation — systematically translating visual flowchart diagrams into structured pseudocode and converting pseudocode algorithms into standard flowcharts

*Notice:* Progresses from foundational logic control structures and step-by-step linear search execution (B7) to complex multi-condition iteration design (B8), culminating in formal algorithmic modeling through flowchart drafting, structured pseudocode, and bidirectional conversion (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 3: Robotics

**Grade 7** *(Robotics Concepts, Laws of Robotics & Societal Applications)*

1. Fundamentals of robotics — definition of a robot, history of automation, Asimov's laws of robotics, and distinguishing autonomous robots from remotely piloted machines
2. Societal applications of robotics — industrial manufacturing (automobile assembly lines), medical surgery, agricultural monitoring/spraying, space exploration, and domestic assistance

**Grade 8** *(The Sense-Think-Act Cycle, Sensors, Controllers & Actuators)*

1. Operating principles of robotics — understanding the foundational Sense-Think-Act feedback loop
2. Anatomy of robotic systems — input sensors (ultrasonic, infrared, light, touch, temperature), microcontrollers/processors (the brain: micro:bit, Arduino, Raspberry Pi), and output actuators (DC motors, servo motors, stepper motors, grippers, LED indicators, buzzers)

**Grade 9** *(Physical Robot Construction & Sensor-Actuator Programming)*

1. Constructing a robot artefact — assembling a functional physical robot model or automated mechanism using lab kits, microcontrollers, or recycled structural materials
2. Programming robot behaviors — writing and downloading control scripts (block-based or micro-Python) to process sensor inputs and drive actuators (e.g. an obstacle-avoiding rover, automated light tracker, or smart hand-sanitizer dispenser)

*Notice:* Progresses from appreciating robot definitions, ethics, and world-wide industrial applications (B7) to decomposing robot anatomy into sensors, microcontrollers, and actuators within the Sense-Think-Act paradigm (B8), culminating in hands-on mechanical fabrication and embedded sensor programming (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 4: Artificial Intelligence

**Grade 7** *(AI Foundations, Human vs Machine Intelligence & Everyday Systems)*

1. Fundamentals of Artificial Intelligence — definition of AI, comparing natural human intelligence with artificial intelligence, and the Turing test benchmark
2. Everyday AI applications — speech-recognition virtual assistants (Siri, Google Assistant, Alexa), facial authentication, predictive text, streaming recommendation engines, and navigation route optimization

**Grade 8** *(Machine Learning Concepts & Artificial Neural Networks)*

1. Machine Learning fundamentals — how AI systems learn from data patterns without explicit line-by-line programming; training data versus testing data
2. Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) — comparing biological brain neurons with artificial nodes; input layer, hidden layers, output layer, weights, and decision thresholds

**Grade 9** *(Knowledge-Based / Expert Systems, Industrial Domains & AI Ethics)*

1. Expert systems architecture — definition of Knowledge-Based Systems, structural components: Knowledge Base (rules/facts), Inference Engine (reasoning mechanism), and User Interface
2. Practical domains of expert systems — medical diagnostic engines (e.g. MYCIN), financial credit scoring, agricultural plant disease diagnostics, and geological/mineral prospecting
3. Ethics and societal implications of AI — algorithmic bias, workforce automation/job displacement, privacy infringement, autonomous decision ethics, and human-in-the-loop governance

*Notice:* Progresses from recognizing AI concepts and interacting with everyday consumer AI applications (B7) to understanding training data dynamics and artificial neural network topologies (B8), culminating in the architectural deconstruction of expert systems, diagnostic inference engines, and crucial ethical/workforce debates (B9).
