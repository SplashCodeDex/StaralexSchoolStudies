Section A: Visual Art
Question 1
Observational drawing of fruit with three shading techniques, then identifying and drawing a line in
nature.
1(a)(i) Three bananas in mass shading [4 marks]
Draw a bunch of three bananas in correct proportion and overlapping naturally.
Render in three tones (light, medium, dark) using mass shading: lay down solid tone and blend
smoothly so no separate lines or dots show.
Show the light source: lightest tone where light hits, darkest tone on the shadow side, medium tone
between.
Mark guide: drawing the bananas (1), three clear tones (2), smooth mass-shading technique (1).
1(a)(ii) An orange in hatching [4 marks]
Draw a single round orange in good proportion.
Render in three tones using hatching: sets of parallel lines; place the lines closer together for
darker areas and further apart for lighter areas.
Keep the lines following the round form of the orange so it looks solid.
Mark guide: drawing the orange (1), three clear tones (2), correct hatching technique (1).
1(a)(iii) Composition of both objects in stippling [4 marks]
Arrange the bananas and the orange together in one balanced composition, overlapping for depth.
Render in three tones using stippling: many small dots; cluster the dots tightly for dark areas and
spread them out for light areas.
Keep one consistent light source across both objects.
Mark guide: composing the objects (1), three clear tones (2), correct stippling technique (1).
1(b)(i) A natural object that is an example of a line [1 mark]
Any natural object seen as a line, for example: the stem or branch of a plant, the veins of a leaf, blades
of grass, a tree root, a spider's web thread, a crack in the ground, or the horizon.
1(b)(ii) Draw the example [2 marks]
Make a clear, simple drawing of the object named in (b)(i), showing its line-like quality (length and
direction).

2(b) Describe three types of packages [6 marks]
Primary package: the wrapper or container that touches the product directly and holds it, such as
a sachet, bottle or wrapper. It protects the item and is the first thing the buyer handles.
Secondary package: the package that holds one or more primary packages together, such as a box
or carton. It groups items, carries branding and information, and protects during display.
Tertiary (transport) package: the large package used for bulk storage and transport, such as a
crate, shipping carton or pallet wrap. It protects many units during distribution.

Cloze passage on the artist El Anatsui, and explaining art appreciation.
3(a) Fill in the blanks [10 marks]
El Anatsui is a renowned Ghanaian sculptor.
He is known for his large-scale sculptures created from discarded materials such as bottle caps.
Anatsui was born into a family of farmers.
He attended the University of Science and Technology and graduated in the year 1969.
His technique involves sorting materials, arranging and rearrange (rearranging) to create new
forms and patterns.
Notably among his works include Broken Bridge.
El Anatsui was awarded an honorary doctorate by the Harvard University.

Section B: Music
Question 4
Features of art music, notes in a 3/4 bar, conducting pattern and triads.
4(a) State and explain two features of art music [4 marks]
It is composed and written down (notated) by a known composer using staff notation, so it can
be performed exactly as written.
It is performed from a score, usually by trained musicians who follow the composer's
instructions on pitch, rhythm and dynamics.
It uses Western harmony, form and instruments such as the choir, organ or orchestra.
It is usually performed in formal concert settings (for example by choirs and orchestras), for
example anthems, hymns and cantatas.
4(b)(i) A different set of notes that fit a 3/4 bar [3 marks]
The time signature is 3/4, so each bar must add up to three crotchet beats.
Any correct combination totalling three crotchet beats is accepted, for example:
a dotted minim (3 beats);
a minim + a crotchet (2 + 1);
a crotchet + four quavers (1 + 2) ... continue to total 3;
six quavers (½ × 6 = 3).
The set drawn must be different from the one given in the question.
4(b)(ii) Suitable conducting pattern for 3/4 [2 marks]
Use the three-beat (triangular) conducting pattern.
The hand moves down on beat 1, out to the right on beat 2, and up back to the start on beat 3
(down – out – up), forming a triangle.
4(c)(i) Triad with C as root, treble clef [3 marks]
A triad is the root, the third above it and the fifth above the root, played together.
On C the triad is C – E – G; notate these three notes stacked on the treble staff (C, the E a third
above, the G a fifth above).

Section B: Music
Question 5
Meaning of a scale, the F major scale, and music appreciation.
5(a) Explain the term scale [3 marks]
A scale is a series of musical notes arranged in order of pitch, going up (ascending) or down
(descending).
The notes follow a fixed pattern of tones and semitones, running from a starting note (the tonic) to
its octave.
5(b) Construct the F major scale, ascending, with key signature [5 marks]
The F major scale ascending is: F – G – A – B♭ – C – D – E – F.
F major has one flat, B flat; show this as the key signature at the start of the staff.
Notate the notes as whole notes (semibreves) on the treble staff, in rising order from F to the F an
octave above.
Mark guide: correct notes in order (3), B flat key signature shown (1), notes correctly placed on
the staff (1).
5(c)(i) Explain music appreciation [3 marks]
Music appreciation is the act of listening to, understanding and judging the qualities and value of a
piece of music.
It involves recognising the elements of the music (such as melody, rhythm and harmony) and
forming an informed opinion about it.
5(c)(ii) Two importance of music appreciation [4 marks]
It develops careful listening skills and a deeper enjoyment of music.
It builds knowledge of musical elements, styles and instruments.
It helps one judge and value performances and compositions fairly.
It helps preserve and promote musical culture and heritage.

6(a) Three reasons folktales should be encouraged [6 marks]
They preserve culture and tradition, passing the customs and beliefs of the people to younger
generations.
They teach morals and values, since most folktales carry lessons about good and bad behaviour.
They entertain the community and bring people together.
They pass on history and knowledge in an easy, memorable way.
They develop language, imagination and creativity in those who tell and watch them.
They provide rich source material for Ghanaian drama and promote national identity.

Section C: Dance Drama
Question 7
Drama terms, and the role of traditional drama in national identity.
7(a) Describe the drama terms [6 marks]
Mood and atmosphere: the feeling or emotional tone of a scene (such as joyful, tense or sad) and
the overall feeling the audience senses; it is built up through lighting, sound, set and acting.
Motivation: the reason or inner drive behind a character's actions; what the character wants and
why they behave the way they do.
Setting: the time and place in which the action of the play happens (the location and the period).
7(b) Three roles of traditional Ghanaian drama in promoting national identity [9 marks]
It preserves and showcases cultural values, customs and language, keeping Ghanaian heritage
alive.
It teaches history and shared heritage, uniting people around common stories and experiences.
It promotes patriotism and pride in being Ghanaian and a sense of belonging.
It reflects the Ghanaian way of life through local symbols, costumes, music and dance that mark
Ghana out as unique.
