# Mathematics — Lesson Plan (Grades 7–9)

*Based on the NaCCA Mathematics Common Core Programme (CCP) Curriculum for JHS1 (B7) – JHS3 (B9).*

**How the subject is grouped.** NaCCA structures Mathematics into four major sessions (Main Strands):
1. **Number** — Place value and numeration systems beyond billions, mental mathematics strategies, integer and multi-digit operations, fractions, decimals, percentages, indices, surds, and ratios, rates, proportions, and financial mathematics.
2. **Algebra** — Patterns and relations (rules, tables of values, linear relations, gradient and equations of straight lines), algebraic expressions (expansion, factorization, algebraic fractions, change of subject), and variables and linear equations/inequalities (one variable, rational coefficients, graphical solutions).
3. **Geometry and Measurement** — 2D and 3D shapes, angle properties (parallel lines, intersecting lines, polygons), geometric constructions (perpendiculars, bisectors, standard angles, triangles, quadrilaterals, inscribed/circumscribed circles, loci), measurement (perimeter, area of triangles and circles, surface area of prisms/cuboids via nets), vectors in 2D (components, magnitude, direction, vector arithmetic, parallel/perpendicular vectors), and bearings (forward and back bearings).
4. **Handling Data** — Data collection methods, questionnaire design and ethical considerations (bias, privacy), organizing and presenting data (frequency tables, line graphs, bar charts, pie charts, stem-and-leaf, waffle diagrams, box-and-whisker plots, histograms), measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and dispersion (range, extreme values/outliers), and theoretical/experimental probability (single events, compound independent events, dependent events with/without replacement, tree diagrams).

**Time allocation (per week, 4 periods of 50 minutes each).** 4 periods (200 minutes total per week), distributed across conceptual exploration, problem-solving demonstrations, collaborative investigation, and independent exercise practice.

**Note.** NaCCA expects Mathematics to be **inquiry-based, hands-on, and problem-oriented**. Learners must manipulate concrete objects, discover mathematical rules through pattern spotting, sketch geometric figures and coordinate graphs accurately, solve authentic Ghanaian socio-economic problems (e.g. market pricing, SSNIT contributions, VAT/NHIL, utilities, local journeys), and communicate mathematical reasoning logically. Theory must directly serve real-world numeracy, critical thinking, and STEM competencies.

# STRAND 1: NUMBER

## Strand 1: Number *(Numeration, Operations, Fractions, Proportions & Financial Math)*

### Sub-strand 1: Number and Numeration Systems

**Grade 7** *(Place Value beyond Billions, Concrete Modeling, Rounding & Decimals)*

1. Modeling large number quantities over 1,000,000,000 using graph sheets, isometric paper, and multi-base blocks
2. Comparing and ordering whole numbers beyond 1,000,000,000 using comparison symbols ($>$, $<$, $=$)
3. Rounding whole numbers over 1,000,000,000 (off, up, down) to nearest hundred-thousand, ten-thousands, thousands, hundreds, and tens
4. Rounding decimals to the nearest tenth, hundredth, and thousandth
5. Expressing decimal numerals to given significant figures and decimal places

**Grade 8** *(Standard Form, Skip Counting, Large Population Problems & Sets of Factors)*

1. Reading and writing numbers over 1,000,000,000 using place value charts and word forms
2. Skip counting forwards and backwards in 10,000s, 100,000s, and 500,000s
3. Comparing and ordering whole numbers in real-world contexts using $>$, $<$, and $=$
4. Expressing integers and large numbers in standard form (scientific notation $A \times 10^n$, where $1 \le A < 10, n \in \mathbb{Z}$)
5. Rounding integers and decimals to specified significant figures and decimal places
6. Formulating and solving real-life word problems involving place value and large populations
7. Identifying perfect squares and determining square roots using sets of factors and prime factorisation
8. Applying set concepts (union $\cup$, intersection $\cap$, universal set $U$) to sets of factors and multiples to solve real-life problems

**Grade 9** *(Rational Number System, Real-Life Budgeting & Set Theory)*

1. Expressing integers of any size to specified significant figures and decimal places in scientific and economic contexts
2. Solving complex real-life problems involving place value (national budgets, census figures, economic allocations)
3. Classifying members of the rational number system ($\mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{W} \subset \mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q}$) and examining non-rational/irrational numbers
4. Applying set theory and Venn diagrams (union, intersection, complement of 2 sets) to classify and solve problems on rational numbers

*Notice:* Progression begins with concrete visual modeling of numbers up to billions and basic rounding rules (B7), develops into scientific standard form, skip counting, square roots, and factor sets (B8), and culminates in formal rational number system hierarchies, Venn diagram set relations, and national-scale numerical modeling (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 2: Number Operations

**Grade 7** *(Mental Strategies, 4-Digit Operations, Index Notation & Prime Factors)*

1. Mental math: Multiplying and dividing numbers by powers of 10, decimals, and benchmark fractions
2. Mental math strategies: Commutative, associative, and distributive properties, compensation, and doubling/halving
3. Applying mental math strategies to solve practical word problems
4. Adding and subtracting up to four-digit whole numbers using standard algorithms and estimation
5. Multiplying and dividing multi-digit numbers by 1-digit and 2-digit numbers
6. Creating and solving real-world story problems involving the four basic operations on decimals
7. Meaning of repeated factors and powers of natural numbers using concrete models (counters, bundle sticks)
8. Expressing numbers as products of prime factors and in index notation ($a^n$)
9. Exponent laws for zero index: Showing that $a^0 = 1$ for any non-zero natural number
10. Evaluating numerical expressions in index form
11. Applying product of prime factors to find Highest Common Factor (HCF) and Least Common Multiple (LCM)

**Grade 8** *(Multi-Digit Operations, Laws of Indices & Exponential Equations)*

1. Mental math: Rapid multiplication and division by powers of 10, decimals, and benchmark fractions
2. Mental math: Applying number properties (distributive, compatible numbers, near doubles) to multi-step arithmetic
3. Mental math: Formulating and solving mental word problems in daily trading and measurements
4. Adding and subtracting multi-digit whole numbers beyond four digits
5. Multiplying and dividing multi-digit numbers by 2-digit and 3-digit numbers using long multiplication/division
6. Creating and solving multi-step word problems involving decimals across all four basic operations
7. Laws of indices: Identifying and explaining the product rule ($a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$), quotient rule ($a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}$), power of a power ($(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$), and negative index ($a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$)
8. Simplifying and evaluating numerical expressions using laws of indices and order of operations (BODMAS/PEMDAS)
9. Solving simple exponential equations where the unknown is an index (e.g. $2^x = 32$, $3^{2x-1} = 27$)
10. Real-life applications of powers and indices (population growth, cell division, computer storage units)

**Grade 9** *(Commutative/Associative/Distributive Properties, Rounding Word Problems, Roots & Surds)*

1. Mental math: Advanced multiplication and division by powers of 10 and benchmark fractions in scientific contexts
2. Operational laws: Demonstrating and applying the commutative ($a+b = b+a$, $ab = ba$), associative ($(a+b)+c = a+(b+c)$), and distributive ($a(b+c) = ab+ac$) properties to simplify calculations
3. Solving multi-step word problems involving all four basic operations and rounding answers to specified significant figures or decimal places
4. Approximating square roots of non-perfect squares using mathematical tables and calculators
5. Surds: Defining and identifying simple surds ($\sqrt{a}$) and compound surds ($a\sqrt{b}$, $\sqrt{a} \pm \sqrt{b}$)
6. Identities and rules of surds: Multiplication rule ($\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}$) and division rule ($\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}$)
7. Simplifying surds to their simplest form ($a\sqrt{b}$ where $b$ is square-free)
8. Addition and subtraction of like surds ($m\sqrt{a} \pm n\sqrt{a} = (m \pm n)\sqrt{a}$)

*Notice:* Arithmetic operations expand from 4-digit algorithms, mental math, and basic prime factor index notation (B7) to multi-digit long operations, formal laws of indices, and exponential equations (B8), reaching surd theory, surd arithmetic, and non-perfect square approximations (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 3: Fractions, Decimals and Percentages

**Grade 7** *(Benchmark Conversions, Fraction Operations & Quantity Sharing)*

1. Recalling and converting benchmark fractions ($\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{5}, \frac{1}{10}$), decimals, and percentages
2. Comparing and ordering common, decimal, and percentage fractions up to thousandths
3. Adding and subtracting unlike fractions and mixed numbers using LCD (Lowest Common Denominator)
4. Solving real-world word problems involving fraction addition and subtraction
5. Multiplying a fraction by a whole number and by another fraction with area/grid models
6. Finding a fraction of a given quantity (money, distances, quantities of objects)
7. Dividing a fraction by a whole number and by a fraction using the reciprocal/invert-and-multiply method
8. Dividing quantities and real-world items by fractions

**Grade 8** *(Operations on Mixed/Negative Fractions & Order of Operations / BODMAS)*

1. Review of operations on positive and negative common fractions and mixed numbers
2. Applying the correct order of operations (BODMAS / PEMDAS) to complex fractions involving brackets, powers, and mixed operators
3. Solving multi-step word problems involving fractions, decimals, and percentages in financial and measurement scenarios

**Grade 9** *(Multi-Step Fractional Modeling, Business Profits & Budget Shares)*

1. Comprehensive review of multi-step fractional operations and complex fractions
2. Order of operations (BODMAS/PEMDAS) applied to nested brackets, negative fractions, and compound operations
3. Advanced real-world problem solving involving fractional shares, business profits, budget allocations, and commercial transactions

*Notice:* Progression builds from foundational benchmark conversions and basic four operations on fractions (B7) to rigorous multi-operator expressions governed by BODMAS/PEMDAS (B8), culminating in multi-step real-world financial, sharing, and commercial fractional calculations (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 4: Number: Ratios and Proportion

**Grade 7** *(Ratio Language, Unit Rates, Ratio Tables & Percentage Rates)*

1. Concept of ratio and using ratio language ($a:b$) to describe relationships between two quantities
2. Concept of unit rate ($\frac{a}{b}$) associated with ratio $a:b$ ($b \ne 0$) and expressing rates in context (speed, cost per item)
3. Constructing tables of equivalent ratios written as common fractions for proportional quantities
4. Using proportional reasoning to find missing values in ratio tables and plotting pairs on the coordinate plane
5. Finding a percentage of a quantity as a rate per hundred ($\frac{r}{100} \times Q$)

**Grade 8** *(Dimensional Unit Conversions, Travel Graphs & Constant of Proportionality)*

1. Using ratio reasoning to convert measurement units (metric length, mass, time, capacity) and dimensional analysis
2. Solving unit rate problems including unit pricing, speed conversions (km/h to m/s), and fuel efficiency
3. Drawing and interpreting distance-time graphs (travel graphs) using the relationship $\text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$
4. Testing and verifying proportional relationships from tables and linear graphs passing through the origin $(0,0)$
5. Identifying the constant of proportionality $k = \frac{y}{x}$ from tables, graphs, equations ($y = kx$), and verbal descriptions

**Grade 9** *(Proportional Equations, Commercial Mathematics & Statutory Deductions / SSNIT)*

1. Formulating algebraic equations for direct and indirect proportional relationships ($y = kx$)
2. Solving multi-step ratio, percentage, and commercial mathematics problems: simple interest ($I = \frac{PRT}{100}$), profit/loss, discounts, sales tax, commissions, and depreciation
3. Financial literacy: Calculating Ghana statutory deductions and benefits including National Health Insurance Levy (NHIL), VAT, and SSNIT Tier 1 and Tier 2 contributions (Act 766 vs PNDC Law 247: employer 13.0%, employee 5.5%)
4. Graphing proportional relationships, interpreting unit rate as graph slope/gradient, and solving economic/scientific problems

*Notice:* Ratio concepts advance from elementary comparisons and unit rates (B7) to unit conversions, distance-time graphs, and constant of proportionality (B8), concluding with commercial math, direct proportionality equations, and Ghanaian civic financial structures (SSNIT, VAT, NHIL) (B9).

# STRAND 2: ALGEBRA

## Strand 2: Algebra *(Patterns, Linear Graphs, Expressions, Equations & Inequalities)*

### Sub-strand 1: Patterns and Relations

**Grade 7** *(Pattern Rules, Mapping Relations, Tables of Values & Coordinate Plotting)*

1. Extending numerical and geometric patterns with and without concrete/symbolic manipulatives
2. Expressing pattern rules verbally using descriptive mathematical phrases (e.g. "one more than twice $n$")
3. Deducing algebraic relations/mapping rules ($x → ax + b$) from numeric or geometric patterns and predicting distant terms
4. Generating tables of values for linear relations and plotting ordered pairs $(x, y)$ on the Cartesian coordinate plane

**Grade 8** *(Gradient of a Line, Slope-Intercept Form $y = mx + c$ & Real-Life Graphs)*

1. Calculating the gradient (slope) of a straight line ($m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$) from coordinate points
2. Writing the equation of a straight line in the slope-intercept form $y = mx + c$
3. Using straight-line graphs to determine missing elements, coordinates, and extrapolate values
4. Interpreting and solving real-life linear graph problems (utility tariffs, cell phone plans, uniform speed)

**Grade 9** *(Pairs of Linear Relations, Simultaneous Graphs & Intersection Points)*

1. Constructing tables of values and graphing pairs of simultaneous linear relations on the same coordinate axes
2. Reading coordinates of intersection points and determining missing ordered pairs from intersecting graphs
3. Solving systems of simultaneous linear equations graphically by finding the common point of intersection $(x, y)$

*Notice:* Progression starts with visual pattern recognition and single-line plotting (B7), develops into analytical coordinate geometry with gradient and $y = mx + c$ (B8), and culminates in graphing pairs of lines to solve simultaneous equations graphically (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 2: Algebraic Expressions

**Grade 7** *(Translation, Collecting Like Terms, Rational Coefficients & Substitution)*

1. Translating written verbal instructions and real-life scenarios into algebraic expressions
2. Adding and subtracting algebraic terms with integer and rational coefficients (collecting like terms)
3. Multiplying and dividing simple algebraic expressions with rational coefficients
4. Evaluating algebraic expressions by numerical substitution
5. Applying arithmetic properties (distributive, commutative, associative) to simplify algebraic expressions

**Grade 8** *(Binomial Expansion, Algebraic Fractions, Substitution & Common Factorisation)*

1. Expanding products of two binomial expressions using the distributive law: $(a + b)(c + d)$ and $(ax + b)(cx + d)$
2. Adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing algebraic expressions containing algebraic fractions
3. Evaluating complex algebraic expressions involving fractions, positive/negative integers, and powers
4. Factorizing algebraic expressions by removing common monomial factors and factorizing by grouping terms

**Grade 9** *(Change of Subject, Quadratic Factorisation & Trinomials)*

1. Changing the subject of a formula in linear, fractional, and radical formulas (e.g. $v = u + at$, $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$, $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$)
2. Evaluating formulas by substituting numerical values for the new subject
3. Factorizing quadratic expressions: difference of two squares ($a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b)$) and quadratic trinomials ($ax^2 + bx + c$)
4. Applying algebraic simplification, expansion, and factorization to solve real-world geometry and physics problems

*Notice:* Moves from basic variable arithmetic, collecting like terms, and simple substitution (B7) to binomial expansion, algebraic fractions, and common grouping factorization (B8), reaching formal change of subject and quadratic factorization (difference of squares and trinomials) (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 3: Variables and Equations

**Grade 7** *(Linear Equations in One Variable, Balance Models & Algebra Tiles)*

1. Translating word problems into linear equations in one variable and translating equations into word stories
2. Modeling and solving one-step linear equations ($x \pm a = b$) using concrete materials (algebra tiles, balance scales)
3. Modeling and solving two-step linear equations ($ax \pm b = c$) symbolically and with algebraic tiles
4. Solving linear equations in one variable algebraically and checking solutions

**Grade 8** *(Linear Inequalities in One Variable & Solution Sets over Domains)*

1. Translating word statements into linear inequalities in one variable ($<, \le, >, \ge$) and vice versa
2. Solving simple linear inequalities in one variable involving addition and subtraction ($x + a \ge b$)
3. Solving simple linear inequalities involving multiplication/division by positive constants and reversing inequality signs for negative multipliers
4. Determining and writing solution sets of linear inequalities over specified replacement sets/domains (whole numbers, integers, real numbers)

**Grade 9** *(Fractional Linear Inequalities, Number Line Graphs & Real-World Modeling)*

1. Solving multi-step linear inequalities in one variable containing rational (fractional) coefficients (e.g. $\frac{2x-1}{3} - \frac{x+2}{4} \le 1$)
2. Illustrating inequality solution sets graphically on a standard horizontal number line with open and solid endpoint circles
3. Formulating and solving comprehensive real-life problems involving linear equations and inequalities

*Notice:* Equations start with linear equations in one variable and balance models (B7), transition to simple linear inequalities and domain solution sets (B8), and expand to fractional-coefficient inequalities, number line representations, and word modeling (B9).

# STRAND 3: GEOMETRY AND MEASUREMENT

## Strand 3: Geometry and Measurement *(Shape, Constructions, Mensuration, Vectors & Bearings)*

### Sub-strand 1: Shape and Space

**Grade 7** *(Angle Classification, Complementary/Supplementary Angles & Bisectors)*

1. Classifying angles by magnitude: acute ($<90°$), right ($90°$), obtuse ($90° < θ < 180°$), straight ($180°$), reflex ($180° < θ < 360°$), and complete revolution ($360°$)
2. Applying properties of complementary angles ($θ_1 + θ_2 = 90°$) and supplementary angles ($θ_1 + θ_2 = 180°$)
3. Solving geometric angle problems using adjacent angles on a straight line and vertically opposite angles
4. Geometric construction: Constructing a perpendicular line from an external point to a line segment using a pair of compasses and straightedge
5. Geometric construction: Constructing the perpendicular bisector of a straight line segment
6. Geometric construction: Copying a given angle and constructing the bisector of an angle
7. Identifying real-world applications of perpendiculars and angle bisectors (architecture, carpentry, road junctions)

**Grade 8** *(Parallel Transversals, Standard Angle Constructions, Triangles & Loci)*

1. Angles formed by parallel lines cut by a transversal: alternate interior angles, corresponding angles, and co-interior (consecutive interior) angles
2. Angle properties of triangles: Sum of interior angles ($180°$) and exterior angle theorem (exterior angle equals sum of two opposite interior angles)
3. Geometric construction: Constructing standard angles with compass and straightedge ($60°, 120°, 90°, 30°, 45°, 75°, 105°, 135°, 150°$)
4. Geometric construction: Constructing triangles (scalene, isosceles, equilateral, right-angled) given side lengths and angle measures (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS)
5. Geometric construction of loci: Locus of points at a fixed distance from a given point (circle); locus of points equidistant from two fixed points (perpendicular bisector); locus of points equidistant from two intersecting lines (angle bisector)

**Grade 9** *(Polygon Angle Formulas, Triangle Similarity/Congruence, Incircle/Circumcircle & Quadrilaterals)*

1. Deriving the interior angle sum formula for an $n$-sided convex polygon ($S = (n-2) × 180°$) and exterior angle sum ($360°$), calculating interior/exterior angles of regular polygons
2. Identifying congruent and similar triangles using geometric criteria (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS for congruence; AAA and proportional sides for similarity)
3. Geometric construction: Inscribing a circle inside a triangle (using angle bisectors to locate the incentre)
4. Geometric construction: Circumscribing a circle about a triangle (using perpendicular bisectors to locate the circumcentre)
5. Geometric construction: Constructing special quadrilaterals (parallelograms, rectangles, squares, rhombuses) under given side, diagonal, and angle conditions

*Notice:* Progression moves from basic angle types, angle pairs, and simple bisectors (B7) to parallel line transversals, construction of standard angles, triangles, and classic loci (B8), culminating in polygon angle formulas, triangle similarity/congruence proofs, incircle/circumcircle constructions, and quadrilateral constructions (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 2: Measurement

**Grade 7** *(Compound-Unit Perimeter, Circumference Deduction, Triangle Area, Bearings & 2D Vectors)*

1. Calculating perimeter of 2D shapes with dimensions in two units (cm and mm, m and cm, km and m) by unit conversion
2. Deducing the relationship between diameter, radius, and circumference of a circle to establish $\pi = \frac{C}{d}$ and $C = 2\pi r$
3. Solving perimeter and circumference problems for circles, semicircles, and composite 2D shapes
4. Drawing rectangles and right-angled triangles on square grids to deduce the area relationship ($A_{\text{triangle}} = \frac{1}{2} b h$)
5. Calculating the area of triangles, parallelograms, and trapeziums in practical contexts
6. Describing three-digit bearings (measured clockwise from North, $000°$ to $360°$)
7. Calculating forward and back bearings using the rule ($\text{Back Bearing} = \theta \pm 180°$)
8. Distinguishing between scalar quantities (magnitude only) and vector quantities (magnitude and direction)
9. Expressing 2D vectors in column (component) form $(x; y)$, calculating magnitude ($|v| = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}$), and determining direction
10. Converting vectors between column form $(x; y)$ and magnitude-bearing form $(k, \theta)$

**Grade 8** *(Circle Area Deduction, Pythagoras Theorem, Basic Trigonometry & Vector Arithmetic)*

1. Deducing the formula for the area of a circle ($A = \pi r^2$) by sector dissection into an approximate parallelogram, and solving circle area problems
2. Proving and establishing the Pythagorean theorem ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$) for right-angled triangles using geometric dissection models
3. Applying the Pythagorean theorem to calculate unknown lengths in right-angled triangles and real-life heights/distances
4. Calculating the area of isosceles and scalene triangles using Pythagoras theorem to find heights
5. Introducing primary trigonometric ratios ($\sin \theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}, \cos \theta = \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}, \tan \theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$) to calculate sides and angles in right-angled triangles
6. Vector arithmetic: Adding and subtracting 2D vectors graphically (head-to-tail) and algebraically in component form
7. Scalar multiplication of vectors ($k(x; y) = (kx; ky)$) and establishing conditions for vector equality

**Grade 9** *(Nets & Surface Area of Prisms, Position Vectors, Parallel/Perpendicular Vectors & Vector Resolution)*

1. Identifying 3D solids (cuboids, cubes, right triangular prisms) and drawing their 2D nets
2. Calculating total surface area of cuboids and cubes using net expansion ($SA = 2(lb + lh + bh)$)
3. Calculating total surface area of right triangular prisms using nets ($SA = 2(\text{base area}) + \text{sum of rectangular faces}$)
4. Representing points in the Cartesian plane as position vectors ($OP = (x; y)$) from the origin $O(0,0)$
5. Conditions for parallel vectors ($u = kv$) and perpendicular vectors (orthogonal dot product / geometric slopes)
6. Applying the Triangle Law and Parallelogram Law of vector addition to resolve resultant displacement and velocity vectors
7. Solving navigational and real-life distance-bearing problems combining vectors and trigonometry

*Notice:* Integrates mensuration with vectors and navigation: circumference, triangle area, 3-digit bearings, and column vectors (B7); circle area, Pythagoras theorem, basic trigonometry ($\sin, \cos, \tan$), and vector arithmetic (B8); 3D nets/surface area of prisms, position vectors, parallel/perpendicular vectors, and triangle/parallelogram vector addition (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 3: Position and Transformation

**Grade 7** *(Reflection, Translation, Coordinate Rules & Reflectional Symmetry)*

1. Identifying shapes and objects in the natural and built environment possessing reflectional (fold/bilateral) symmetry
2. Plotting points and 2D polygons on the coordinate plane and constructing their images under reflection in the $x$-axis ($y=0$), $y$-axis ($x=0$), and coordinate lines $x=k$ or $y=k$
3. Constructing images of plane figures under translation by a displacement vector $(a; b)$ where $(x'; y') = (x+a; y+b)$
4. Verifying properties of reflection and translation (isometry: preserves lengths, angles, area) and identifying congruent and similar figures in kente cloth, floor tiles, and architectural patterns

**Grade 8** *(Rotation, Centre of Rotation, Angles of Rotation & Congruence)*

1. Defining rotation and identifying rotation in everyday life (wheel rotation, fan blades, steering wheels, clock hands)
2. Determining center of rotation $(a, b)$, direction (clockwise / counter-clockwise), and angle of rotation ($\pm 90°, 180°, 270°$)
3. Plotting and constructing rotation images of points and shapes on the coordinate plane about the origin $(0,0)$
4. Investigating congruency of figures under rotation (isometry preserving size and shape)

**Grade 9** *(Enlargement, Scale Factors, Area Scale & Geometric Similarity)*

1. Identifying real-life examples of enlargement/reduction (photo resizing, microscope magnification, projectors, architectural blue prints)
2. Performing geometric enlargement on 2D shapes on graph sheets given a centre of enlargement $(a, b)$ and a scale factor $k$ (positive, negative, and fractional)
3. Determining coordinates of enlarged shapes algebraically: $(x', y') = (k(x-a)+a, k(y-b)+b)$
4. Investigating the geometric properties of enlarged figures: angle preservation, scale factor of lengths ($k$), and scale factor of area ($k^2$), establishing similarity versus congruence

*Notice:* Systematically journeys through the 4 Euclidean transformations: Reflection and Translation (B7), Rotation (B8), and Enlargement / Similarity (B9).

# STRAND 4: HANDLING DATA

## Strand 4: Handling Data *(Statistics, Data Analysis, Representation & Probability)*

### Sub-strand 1: Data

**Grade 7** *(Qualitative vs Quantitative Data, Questionnaire Design, Bar Charts & Mean/Median)*

1. Distinguishing between qualitative (categorical) and quantitative (numerical) data, selecting and justifying appropriate collection methods
2. Designing, refining, and administering a questionnaire to gather primary data on school or community questions
3. Organizing raw survey data into frequency tally tables and presenting data in simple bar charts and pictographs
4. Calculating and interpreting the mean ($\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n}$) for ungrouped data sets
5. Determining the median (middle value of an ordered array) for ungrouped data sets

**Grade 8** *(Grouped/Ungrouped Data, Diverse Charts, Stem-and-Leaf, Box Plots & Measures of Central Tendency)*

1. Classifying data types: numerical (discrete vs continuous), categorical (nominal vs ordinal), ungrouped, and grouped data
2. Selecting and justifying primary vs secondary data collection methods to investigate a specific statistical inquiry
3. Constructing frequency distribution tables and drawing statistical charts: line graphs, pie charts ($360°$ sector angle conversions), bar graphs, pictographs, stem-and-leaf plots, waffle diagrams, and box-and-whisker plots
4. Calculating the mean, median, and mode for ungrouped data sets and comparing their values
5. Evaluating and justifying the most appropriate measure of central tendency (mean, median, or mode) in specific real-world contexts (e.g. median for skewed salaries, mode for shoe inventory, mean for test scores)

**Grade 9** *(Ethical Data Collection, Continuous Grouped Data, Histograms & Outlier Analysis)*

1. Formulating research questions and evaluating data collection methodologies, considering statistical bias, ethical considerations, timing, privacy, language clarity, and cultural sensitivities
2. Organizing grouped and ungrouped continuous data into class intervals, calculating class midpoints, class boundaries, and frequencies
3. Constructing histograms with equal class intervals and using the modal bar/cross lines to estimate the mode graphically
4. Calculating comprehensive descriptive statistics: minimum, maximum, range ($R = \text{max} - \text{min}$), mean, median, and mode
5. Analyzing and demonstrating the effect of extreme values (outliers) on the mean, median, and mode of a data set

*Notice:* Data concepts advance from basic qualitative/quantitative surveys, bar charts, and mean/median (B7) to diverse graphical displays (pie charts, stem-and-leaf, box plots), grouped tables, and selecting the optimal average (B8), culminating in ethical data collection, histograms for modal estimation, full descriptive statistics, and outlier distortion analysis (B9).

---

### Sub-strand 2: Chance or Probability

**Grade 7** *(Likelihood Scale, Single Events & Sample Spaces)*

1. Concept of chance and likelihood: Providing personal and real-world examples of events that are impossible, possible/unlikely/likely, and certain
2. Classifying outcomes on the probability scale from $0$ (impossible) to $1$ (certain) or $0\%$ to $100\%$
3. Defining sample space for single-stage random experiments (e.g. tossing a fair coin, rolling a fair 6-sided die, picking a card)
4. Calculating experimental and theoretical probabilities of single events as fractions, decimals, percentages, and ratios: $P(E) = \frac{n(E)}{n(S)}$

**Grade 8** *(Compound Independent Events, Outcome Grids & Tree Diagrams)*

1. Conducting multi-stage probability experiments involving two independent events (e.g. tossing two coins, rolling two dice, drawing colored bottle tops with replacement)
2. Listing outcomes and constructing sample space tables (outcome grids) and tree diagrams for two independent events
3. Calculating probabilities of compound independent events using the multiplication rule ($P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P(B)$) and expressing results as fractions, decimals, percentages, and odds/ratios

**Grade 9** *(Compound Dependent Events, Without Replacement & Conditional Tree Diagrams)*

1. Conducting multi-stage probability experiments involving two dependent events (e.g. drawing items without replacement from a bag, sequential selection)
2. Constructing probability tree diagrams and two-way contingency tables for dependent compound events, updating conditional probabilities along branches
3. Calculating probabilities of compound dependent events and applying probability modeling to real-world risk, quality control, and decision making

*Notice:* Probability progresses systematically: Single-event likelihood scale and sample spaces (B7) to two independent events with replacement and tree diagrams (B8), culminating in dependent events without replacement and conditional branching trees (B9).
